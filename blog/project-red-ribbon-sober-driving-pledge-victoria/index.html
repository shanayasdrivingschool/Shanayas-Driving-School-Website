Project Red Ribbon: A Sober-Driving Pledge for Victoria Learners and Families

A practical sober-driving plan for Victoria learners and families, based on MADD Canada’s Project Red Ribbon and current ICBC guidance.

By Shanaya's Driving School · Published November 1, 2026 · 4 min read · Driver Safety

MADD Canada's Project Red Ribbon is a national holiday-season campaign reminding Canadians to never drive impaired and inviting people to take a sober-driving pledge. The campaign page does not establish a November 1 launch date, so this article uses the confirmed holiday-season message without assigning an unverified launch date.

Make the plan before the celebration

If your plans involve alcohol, cannabis or another drug, decide how you will get home before you go out. ICBC recommends leaving the car at home, taking transit or a taxi or ride-hailing service, or choosing a designated driver who will stay sober.

For learners and novice drivers, the decision is even clearer. ICBC says L and N drivers must have zero alcohol and zero drugs in their blood while driving. If there is any doubt about your ability to drive safely, do not drive.

A simple sober-driving plan

  1. Choose the person who will drive before the first drink or other intoxicant.
  2. Arrange a backup: transit, a taxi, a ride-hailing service, a friend or an overnight stay.
  3. Leave the vehicle at home if you might not be able to drive safely later.
  4. Keep the keys away from anyone who is impaired.
  5. If a friend has used alcohol, cannabis or another drug, help them find a safe ride without shaming them.

Coffee, food, a short nap or waiting for a particular number of hours does not turn an impaired driver into a safe driver.

The pledge

I will never drive impaired. I will plan a safe ride home, help my friends do the same and keep my keys away from anyone who cannot drive safely.

Take the official pledge through MADD Canada's Project Red Ribbon. For current B.C. guidance, read ICBC's pages on alcohol-impaired drivingand drug-impaired driving.

A learner's perspective on safe habits

In a saved review snapshot, Violet Lily wrote that her instructor helped her “develop safe defensive driving habits.” That is one learner's experience, not evidence of a typical outcome or a substitute for the sober-driving plan above.

If you want structured practice with defensive habits, see our defensive-driving course or contact the school with questions about the current service, instructor and availability. No lesson replaces the decision to leave driving to a sober person when you are impaired.
