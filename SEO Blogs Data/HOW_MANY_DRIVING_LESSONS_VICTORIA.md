# How Many Driving Lessons Do You Need Before a Victoria Driving Test?

Prepared by Shanaya’s Driving School

You’re comparing lesson packages, looking at your budget and wondering whether five lessons will be enough before your Victoria driving test. Booking ten might feel reassuring, but you want to know what you actually need.

**There is no single lesson count that makes every learner ready.** ICBC’s [Class 7 preparation guidance](https://www.icbc.com/driver-licensing/new-drivers/Get-your-N) recommends supervised practice and suggests driver training without setting a fixed paid-lesson total. Your starting skills and practice opportunities matter. An assessment lesson can help you turn that uncertainty into a plan.

This guide focuses on the **Class 7 road test for your novice, or N, licence** in Victoria, British Columbia. Confirm your own ICBC requirements if you are taking another test or transferring a licence.

## Does ICBC require a minimum number of driving lessons?

The published guidance does not prescribe a universal lesson package. You still need to meet ICBC’s [learner-stage conditions](https://www.icbc.com/driver-licensing/new-drivers/Get-your-N). Being eligible to take the test and being comfortable making safe decisions are separate milestones.

A separate [ICBC-approved Graduated Licensing Program (GLP) course](https://www.icbc.com/driver-licensing/driver-training/New-drivers-or-riders) has its own hours: at least 16 classroom, 12 on-road and four flexible hours. Those describe that course format, not a requirement for every Class 7 candidate.

> “Professional training can help you learn faster and avoid developing bad driving habits.”

ICBC, [Learn to Drive Smart, page 135](https://www.icbc.com/assets/en/40I9m5k2Tqnf7aNs5kqPHj/drivers9.pdf#page=1).

## What does ICBC’s 60-hour practice recommendation mean?

ICBC’s [Learn to Drive Smart manual, page 139](https://www.icbc.com/assets/en/40I9m5k2Tqnf7aNs5kqPHj/drivers9.pdf#page=5) recommends **at least 60 hours of practice** before the Class 7 road test. This is a practice recommendation, not a requirement to buy 60 lessons. Reaching that total also does not automatically mean you are ready to pass.

Keep a record of driving time with your instructor and qualified supervisor, listing them separately. Follow your learner restrictions on every drive. Alongside the minutes, note what became easier and where you still needed help.

For example, a note about needing observation reminders while parking gives your next lesson a useful starting point. ICBC’s [Tuning up for Drivers guide](https://www.icbc.com/driver-licensing/driving-guides/Tuning-Up-for-Drivers) provides a structure for working through driving skills with your supervisor.

## Are five or ten driving lessons enough?

Imagine two learners buying five sessions. One has practised regularly with family; the other has barely driven. They are buying the same number of appointments, but starting with very different needs.

> “Even one or two lessons can make a difference in passing your road test.”

ICBC, [road-test tips, July 21, 2022](https://www.icbc.com/about-icbc/newsroom/2022-jul-21).

That is encouragement to get useful instruction, not a promise that a beginner can prepare in two lessons. Ask what each session should help you improve.

### Compare instruction hours as well as lesson counts

- **Five 60-minute lessons:** five hours of instruction.
- **Five 90-minute lessons:** seven and a half hours of instruction.
- **Ten 90-minute lessons:** fifteen hours of instruction.

These are calculations, not recommended totals. Ask how much appointment time is spent driving and what feedback is included. Our [Victoria lesson-cost guide](https://www.shanayasdrivingschool.com/blog/driving-lessons-cost-victoria-bc-2026) can help you compare spending.

### Ask for a reason behind each additional lesson

If another lesson is suggested, ask what it will address. A specific goal, such as working on gap choices at intersections, gives you something to track. Agree on what to practise with your supervisor and when to review progress.

## What should your lessons prepare you for in Victoria?

A familiar journey can feel easy until the signs or traffic change. The [City of Victoria’s speed-limit update](https://www.victoria.ca/getting-around/driving/lower-speed-limits) says local-street reductions to 30 km/h were completed in December 2025, with most major streets moving to 40 km/h during 2026, subject to exceptions. This concerns the City of Victoria, not every municipality in Greater Victoria.

## How do you know you’re getting closer to test-ready?

Ask your instructor to discuss the five areas in ICBC’s [Road Test Skills Explainer](https://www.icbc.com/assets/en/49OS8RJMWgWKgOx4U0EGOa/skills-explainer.pdf): observation, space margin, speed, steering and communication. Make the feedback specific:

- **Observation:** Do I notice hazards and make the necessary checks without reminders?
- **Space margin:** Are my following distances, road position and gap choices appropriate?
- **Speed:** Do I respond to limits and conditions consistently?
- **Steering:** Is my control reliable through turns and manoeuvres?
- **Communication:** Are my signals timely and clear to other road users?

Use these questions for discussion, not self-scoring. Ask whether you still need coaching or safety interventions, and whether improvements hold across different drives. A [mock test evaluation](https://www.shanayasdrivingschool.com/courses/mock-test-evaluation) can reveal remaining work; it cannot guarantee a pass.

## How to plan lessons without buying more than you need

1. **Explain your starting point.** Share your experience, practice access, budget and any planned test date.
2. **Ask for an initial assessment.** Request specific feedback and priorities for your next sessions.
3. **Agree on a review point.** Decide when you will revisit the plan instead of treating the first estimate as a fixed total.
4. **Keep a practice record.** Bring questions and recurring difficulties to the next lesson.
5. **Confirm the purchase details.** Get lesson duration, inclusions, fees and cancellation terms in writing.

ICBC’s [school-selection guidance](https://www.icbc.com/driver-licensing/driver-training/Choosing-your-driving-school) recommends checking school and instructor licensing and requesting written service and policy details. Clear terms help you compare instruction on the same basis.

## Frequently asked questions about lesson numbers

### Are five driving lessons enough before a Victoria road test?

They may help an experienced learner address specific gaps. For a beginner, five lessons should not be treated as a promise of readiness. Ask an instructor what you can do consistently and what still needs practice before deciding whether to book more.

### Does ICBC recommend 60 driving lessons?

No. Its Learn to Drive Smart manual recommends at least 60 hours of practice before the Class 7 road test. That means practice time, not 60 purchased lessons, and reaching the total does not guarantee a pass.

### How many more lessons should I take after failing a road test?

Bring your examiner’s feedback to an instructor and work out what needs attention. One specific difficulty and several recurring problems call for different plans. Reassess those skills before your next attempt rather than choosing an arbitrary number of extra lessons.
