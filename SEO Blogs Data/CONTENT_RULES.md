# SEO content rules for Shanaya’s Driving School

## Basis and scope

Based on the supplied [Search Quality Evaluator Guidelines](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md), dated **September 11, 2025** in the document. References below use its section numbers and original PDF page anchors. This is an adaptation of that supplied edition, not a claim that it is the latest edition.

Apply these rules to SEO research, article briefs, blog posts, FAQs, service-page copy, author information, and related content metadata. They are project editorial requirements. Specific workflows, source logs, reviewer requirements, and article structures below are our implementation choices, not prescribed Google templates or ranking requirements.

Search quality ratings evaluate search results and help improve search systems; an individual rating does not directly change a page’s ranking. Do not promise rankings, describe this checklist as a Google score, or present E-E-A-T as a numerical target. See [§0.1, p. 6](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-6).

## How to apply these rules

- For new content, use the brief in section 10 before drafting. For a focused edit, inspect the affected claims and nearby context; do not require a new full article brief for a spelling correction or similarly limited change.
- Check the existing article inventory and relevant draft notes before choosing a new page. [The article status audit](ARTICLE_STATUS_AUDIT.md) is a dated snapshot, not a live inventory; inspect the current source and, when publication status matters, the live page. Prefer updating an existing article when it serves the same reader need; create a separate page when its purpose is meaningfully different.
- Read [company knowledge](../COMPANY_KNOWLEDGE.md) for business context and follow its references to the relevant records. It summarizes website content and includes known conflicts; it is not independent verification of every statement it contains.
- When using student feedback to plan content or support school claims, read [saved reviews](reviews.md) and apply section 7.1. Use the relevant learner concerns and concrete experiences; do not insert testimonials into every article.
- Treat explicit user instructions as authoritative for task scope and requested changes. For factual claims, record the supporting evidence separately; a requested phrase does not by itself establish an external credential, legal rule, or third-party endorsement.
- Keep briefs, claim logs, and unresolved questions in working notes beside the draft or in the task handoff. Do not expose internal scores, verification placeholders, or editorial instructions in public copy or metadata.
- Continue all independent drafting and editing when evidence is missing. Ask only for information needed to resolve a material gap that available sources cannot answer. Do not invent a review or add an approval step for routine work.
- Report what changed, what was actually checked, and any remaining evidence, subject-review, or rendering gaps. Apply the readiness statuses in section 10 to the content actually reviewed.

These application steps are project practices, not additional Google requirements. They apply to content work, including public copy embedded in application files, and do not govern unrelated application development.

## 1. Give each page a clear purpose and reader

- State the primary reader, question, and useful outcome in the brief. Distinguish beginners, parents, anxious adults, newcomers, and drivers preparing for different licence classes when their needs differ.
- Identify whether the reader wants an answer, a comparison, booking help, or a local service. Fulfil that intent before promoting a lesson.
- Answer the central question near the beginning. A cost article must provide verified prices or clearly explained cost information; a vehicle-checklist article must make the checklist easy to find.
- Keep the scope focused. Cover supporting questions that help the reader complete the task; link to separate topics when useful.
- Judge usefulness for Victoria, British Columbia. Use Canadian spelling, explain licensing abbreviations, and identify currency and units where needed.

Basis: [§2.2, p. 10](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-10), [§5.2.2, p. 62](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-62), [§12.0–12.2, pp. 94–95](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-94), [§17.0, p. 154](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-154).

## 2. Apply higher standards where errors could cause harm

- Treat driving-safety instructions and licence-eligibility guidance as content requiring high accuracy and appropriate expertise. The source explicitly includes qualifications for obtaining a driver’s licence in its YMYL discussion.
- Assess the actual claim and possible harm; do not treat every casual driving-school update as equally sensitive.
- Verify licensing rules, restrictions, exemptions, deadlines, test requirements, and insurance claims against current authoritative sources before describing them as fact.
- Distinguish a legal requirement, official guidance, an instructor’s recommendation, and an illustrative example. Never turn recommended practice hours into a legal minimum without evidence.
- Give applicable conditions and exceptions alongside the main answer. Do not generalize one learner’s eligibility to everyone.
- Obtain review by an appropriately qualified instructor for practical driving instruction. Use the relevant official authority and, when needed, appropriately qualified review for licensing or insurance interpretations beyond that instructor’s expertise.
- Drafting may proceed while review is pending. Record the missing review or evidence; do not label the article ready to publish until essential safety and eligibility claims are resolved.

Basis: [§2.3, pp. 11–12](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-11), [§3.4–3.4.1, pp. 26–28](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-26), [§11.0, p. 92](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-92).

## 3. Make important claims traceable

Use sources according to what they can establish:

| Claim | Evidence to use |
| --- | --- |
| Licensing rules, test policies, eligibility, government changes | Current ICBC material, Province of British Columbia announcements, or applicable official legislation; match effective dates and scope |
| School prices, services, promotions, pickup areas, policies | Current school records or confirmed business information; reconcile discrepancies with published pages |
| Competitor prices or services | The competitor’s dated first-party pages, compared on equivalent terms |
| Independent reputation | Credible external reviews or reporting, read in context; distinguish independent sources from business-controlled promotion |
| Teaching observations | Actual instructor input or documented experience, accurately attributed |
| Learner concerns and personal experiences | Forums, community discussions, and authentic student accounts; use these for experience and intent, not as authority for legal rules |

- Open and inspect sources. Search snippets, AI summaries, source labels, and citations copied from another article are not verification.
- In the working notes, record each consequential claim, its source URL or internal record, relevant evidence, verification date, effective date if applicable, and any unresolved conditions.
- Link public factual claims to the specific supporting page near the claim. Do not cite a homepage when a policy page is available. Keep private records out of public links.
- Resolve conflicting evidence by checking authority, recency, effective date, and applicability. State uncertainty when it remains; omit an unsupported optional claim or mark an essential one unresolved in the draft.
- When company notes, source code, booking information, and published pages disagree, record the discrepancy and check the underlying business record or applicable dated owner instruction. Do not silently choose the most favourable price or claim, or assume an existing page proves that a service is currently available.
- Never invent statistics, source URLs, quotations, test pass rates, reviewer credentials, or first-hand experience.
- Separate observed results, attributed recommendations, and illustrative calculations. A lesson-count range or claim about what learners "typically" need requires evidence appropriate to that population. Show units and assumptions in budget or lesson-duration calculations; a worked example is not a recommended total or evidence of test readiness.
- Previous editorial notes can identify sources and checks already performed. Attribute those checks to their recorded date and scope; do not report them as checks performed in the current task. Reinspect time-sensitive or disputed evidence when the task requires current facts.

Basis: [§3.2–3.3, pp. 21–23](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-21), [§3.4, pp. 26–27](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-26). The source-log format is a project practice.

## 4. Show real responsibility and expertise

- Identify the actual author or responsible organization. Link to accurate author or school information when available.
- Add a reviewer’s name, qualifications, review scope, and review date only when that person actually performed the review. A byline or badge alone does not establish trust.
- Use documented teaching experience and useful explanations to demonstrate expertise. Attribute instructor observations; do not make up student stories or write “we see this every week” without evidence.
- Distinguish school licensing, instructor licensing, directory inclusion, and approval of a particular training program. Verify the precise status before making claims.
- The project README identifies Shanaya’s as an independent school and cautions that directory inclusion is not ICBC approval, recommendation, or endorsement. Preserve that distinction; verify current status before publishing credential claims.
- Make the business’s identity, contact options, and relevant booking, cancellation, refund, and payment policies easy to find.

Basis: [§2.5.2–2.5.3, pp. 16–18](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-16), [§3.4, pp. 26–27](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-26), [§5.5–5.6, p. 63](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-63). Project context: [README](../README.md).

## 5. Add useful original work

- Each article should contribute something useful beyond a paraphrase: a carefully sourced decision guide, a practical checklist, an explained price comparison, verified local examples, or genuine instructor insight.
- Explain how the information helps the learner decide or act. Adding “Victoria” to generic advice is insufficient localization.
- Use local roads, test-centre details, service boundaries, and pickup arrangements only when verified. Label hypothetical practice scenarios as examples.
- Do not present a practice route as an official or guaranteed test route, suggest insider knowledge of examiners, or promise a pass.
- Use AI for assistance when helpful, with factual checking and editorial review. AI use alone does not determine quality; publishing many unoriginal pages with little added value is the concern.
- Do not copy competitors, lightly rewrite their posts, stitch together sources without useful synthesis, or produce near-identical pages by swapping neighbourhood names.
- Use visuals when they clarify a manoeuvre, comparison, or process. Illustrations must not misrepresent road rules or imply authentic student or instructor photography when they are synthetic.

Basis: [§3.2, p. 21](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-21), [§4.6.5–4.6.6, p. 42](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-42), [§7.1, p. 72](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-72), [§8.1, p. 78](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-78).

## 6. Write clearly and make accurate promises

- Use a descriptive title that matches the answer delivered. Avoid exaggerated claims, fabricated urgency, “secret” routes, and guaranteed outcomes.
- Use natural query language in titles and headings when it helps readers. Do not impose keyword-density targets, repeat location phrases mechanically, or pad text to meet an SEO word count.
- Set length by the task. Remove generic introductions, repeated conclusions, unrelated FAQs, and sections that add no useful information.
- Use short paragraphs, descriptive headings, and tables or lists when they improve comprehension. Explain conditions where the reader needs them.
- Include only relevant FAQs and answer them directly. There is no required FAQ count or universal article length in these project rules.
- Metadata must accurately describe the article. A date or claim in the title or description needs the same support as the body.
- Keep page titles, descriptions, social previews, image captions, and structured data consistent with the visible content. Do not add invented authors, reviews, ratings, credentials, offers, or review dates to metadata.
- Use descriptive internal links to relevant existing pages and confirmed booking destinations. Avoid forced keyword anchors, arbitrary link quotas, and links that imply an unavailable service.
- Write respectfully for anxious learners, older adults, newcomers, and parents. Avoid shaming, stereotypes, or unsupported claims to treat anxiety.

Basis: [§3.1–3.2, pp. 20–21](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-20), [§5.2–5.2.2, pp. 60–62](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-60), [§12.2, p. 95](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-95). Keyword, formatting, metadata-consistency, and internal-link practices here are editorial choices.

## 7. Keep commercial content honest and usable

- Make clear that Shanaya’s publishes the content and offers paid lessons. Do not frame the school’s own recommendation as an independent review.
- For prices, state lesson duration, currency, inclusions, relevant extra charges, tax treatment when verified, and applicable conditions. Compare equivalent services.
- For discounts, verify the actual offer, eligibility, covered products, dates, exclusions, and how to redeem it. “Up to” savings must not imply every learner receives the maximum.
- Use genuine testimonials faithfully and with appropriate permission. Do not create fake reviews, selectively rewrite their meaning, or infer fraud from a handful of ratings.
- For supplied review excerpts, record the reviewer attribution, exact source text, and available source link in working notes. Mark omissions without changing the meaning; do not complete truncated text, merge different students' experiences, or treat owner replies as independent testimony. If only a saved copy was inspected, say so in the notes rather than claiming live verification.
- Treat individual learner outcomes as individual experiences. Reviews do not establish average lesson counts, pass rates, instructor credentials, current offers, or guaranteed results. Verify aggregate ratings and review counts separately before presenting them as current, and do not omit material qualifications when summarizing a mixed review.
- Describe competitors fairly using current evidence. An absence of visible information is not proof that a service is unavailable or poor.
- Lack of independent reputation information does not by itself establish low quality for a small local school.
- Place booking links where they help the next step. Keep the main answer accessible; avoid obstructive overlays, misleading buttons, and repeated sales interruptions.
- Check the rendered page on mobile: headings, tables, source links, and booking/contact links must be readable and usable. Evaluate content and functionality, not appearance alone.

Basis: [§3.3.2, p. 23](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-23), [§3.3.5, p. 25](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-25), [§3.4, p. 27](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-27), [§4.5.3–4.5.4, pp. 36–38](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-36), [§5.3, p. 62](../searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.md#pdf-page-62).

## 8. Keep time-sensitive information current

- Verify current policy and pricing at drafting and again before publication when they may have changed. Distinguish proposed, announced, and effective changes.
- Record the actual published, updated, and reviewed dates according to what occurred. Do not refresh a visible date without a substantive update or accurately described review.
- Set review reminders for policy effective dates and promotion expiries. This is an editorial requirement; reminders are not automatically scheduled by this file.
- Correct affected titles, summaries, FAQs, tables, related pages, and metadata when an underlying fact changes.
- Keep historical information clearly dated. Remove expired offer language or replace it with a verified current offer.

## 10. Use a short brief and a separate final review

Before writing, record:

1. Primary reader, target question, location, and intended outcome.
2. Working title and page scope; overlap with existing articles.
3. Important claims, authoritative sources, and verification gaps.
4. Useful original contribution and any real instructor input needed.
5. Actual author/responsible organization, required review, and a relevant next step.
6. Dates or events that will require a future content check.

For consequential claims, use this compact evidence log or an equivalent format. Omit fields that do not apply; never fill a missing field with an invented source or date.

| Claim and draft location | Inspected source URL or private record reference | Supporting evidence and conditions | Checked on / effective from | Gap or required review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exact claim and section | Specific supporting page or internal record | What the source establishes, including scope | Actual check date; effective date if applicable | Unresolved evidence or review, or none |

Before calling content ready to publish, check:

- [ ] The title, introduction, and body fulfil the same reader need.
- [ ] Consequential facts have inspected supporting evidence and applicable conditions.
- [ ] Safety, licensing, pricing, and promotion uncertainties are resolved or the unsupported material is removed without defeating the page’s purpose.
- [ ] Required subject review occurred and is accurately recorded.
- [ ] Identity, credentials, experience, reviews, and commercial claims are truthful.
- [ ] Quotations preserve their source meaning; numerical examples have correct arithmetic, stated assumptions, and no unsupported implication of typical or guaranteed results.
- [ ] If reviews are used, customer text is separated from owner replies and interface labels; attribution, material criticism, incomplete text, and individual-outcome limits follow section 7.1. Current offers and aggregate ratings have separate verification.
- [ ] The page adds useful original explanation or functionality and contains no substantial filler.
- [ ] Local details and advertised services are verified.
- [ ] Dates, sources, and maintenance needs are recorded accurately.
- [ ] Titles, descriptions, social previews, and any structured data agree with the visible content; internal drafting notes are absent from public output.
- [ ] The rendered page and relevant links work on mobile; if only a draft was reviewed, report that rendering remains unchecked.
- [ ] Booking prompts support the reader’s next step without obstructing the answer.

Record two separate judgments: **page quality** (trust, accuracy, originality, usability) and **intent satisfaction** (does this page help this reader answer this query?). A trustworthy page can still answer the wrong question. Do not label every broad article “Fully Meets”; the supplied guidelines reserve that category for specific, clear, unambiguous intent and a result that completely satisfies all or almost all users in the relevant locale. This can include a particular website, page, or simple fact.

Use one of these statuses with concrete reasons and remaining work:

| Status | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| **Ready** | All applicable checks passed, essential evidence and required subject review are complete, and the rendered page and relevant links were checked. This status does not itself publish the page. |
| **Needs revision** | Known copy, intent, consistency, link, or rendering problems remain. State the specific fixes needed. |
| **Draft awaiting evidence/review** | Drafting is complete as far as available information allows, but essential evidence, required subject review, or final rendering checks remain. Name each outstanding check explicitly. |

These are internal editorial statuses, not Google ratings. Missing evidence for one claim should not stop unrelated drafting, and this checklist is not a new permission requirement for ordinary edits. For a limited edit, report the checks performed on that edit without implying the whole page passed this review.

Record publication state separately from editorial readiness: **draft only**, **implemented locally**, or **live confirmed**, with the file or URL and actual inspection date. Use **publication unverified** when the available evidence cannot establish it. A page can be live and still need revision; content that is Ready locally is not thereby published. Public availability does not establish search indexing or ranking. Keep Markdown review copies and implemented article text consistent when both are maintained; identify which version was reviewed.
