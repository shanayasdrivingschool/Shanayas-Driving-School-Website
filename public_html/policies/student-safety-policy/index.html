Student Safety Policy

Student protection, physical contact, emergency intervention and professional conduct policy, including reporting concerns and fair review.

Published policy

Effective: April 1, 2026

Shanaya's Driving School's Student Protection, Physical Contact, Emergency Intervention & Professional Conduct Policy sets out standards for student and instructor safety, personal boundaries and fair handling of concerns. Policy SDS-SAF-001, version 1.0; policy owner: School Management.

  • Personal boundaries and emergency intervention
  • Reporting concerns without retaliation
  • Fair investigation and privacy safeguards

1. Purpose

1.1 Shanaya's Driving School (the "School") is committed to safe, professional, respectful and legally compliant driver training.

1.2 This Policy establishes standards for personal boundaries, physical contact, emergency safety intervention, student and instructor protection, incident documentation, complaint investigation, privacy, and organizational accountability.

1.3 It aims to prevent misconduct; protect students' physical safety, dignity and autonomy; safeguard instructors against arbitrary findings; and support fair, evidence-based decisions.

1.4 No provision authorizes conduct prohibited by applicable law, ICBC requirements or licensing conditions.

2. Scope

2.1 This Policy applies to instructors, employees, contractors, authorized representatives, students and, where relevant, parents or guardians.

2.2 It applies to driving lessons, introductory or free-trial sessions, mock assessments, road-test preparation, pickup and drop-off, school-related electronic communications and related activities, including at ICBC facilities.

2.3 Instructors shall receive and acknowledge this Policy before providing instruction. Students shall receive reasonable notice of relevant provisions before instruction begins.

2.4 This Policy shall be applied consistently, subject to individual circumstances and required accommodation.

3. Governing Requirements

3.1 This Policy shall be interpreted consistently with the BC Motor Vehicle Act and Motor Vehicle Act Regulations (including Division 27); BC Human Rights Code; Personal Information Protection Act; Child, Family and Community Service Act; applicable provisions of the Criminal Code of Canada; occupational health and safety obligations; and ICBC's Driving School Code of Conduct.

3.2 Mandatory law and regulatory rules prevail over an inconsistent provision of this Policy.

3.3 No acknowledgment or signature waives a statutory right or legal obligation.

3.4 This Policy supplements, and does not replace, ICBC's official Code of Conduct and required school policies.

  • ICBC Driving School Code of Conduct

4. Definitions

4.1 Physical contact: Direct bodily contact between persons, whether intentional or accidental.

4.2 Prohibited physical contact: Contact unnecessary for legitimate, lawful instruction, emergency intervention, accommodation or other proper purpose, or contact that is sexual, coercive, abusive, punitive, intimidating or otherwise inappropriate.

4.3 Incidental contact: Unintended contact arising without a deliberate attempt to touch another person.

4.4 Emergency safety intervention: Action responding to an actual or reasonably perceived imminent threat of collision, injury or immediate danger.

4.5 Reasonable intervention: An action reasonably necessary and proportionate to the risk as it appeared at the time, accounting for available alternatives and urgency.

4.6 Professional boundaries: Appropriate physical, verbal, social, emotional and electronic conduct within the instructor-student relationship.

4.7 Complaint: A report or concern about suspected misconduct, unsafe conduct or policy non-compliance.

4.8 Complainant / respondent: Respectively, the person reporting a concern and the person whose conduct is questioned.

4.9 Minor: A person under 19 years old for the purposes of this Policy.

4.10 Retaliation: Improper adverse treatment because a person made, considered making, or participated in a good-faith complaint, disclosure or investigation.

4.11 Knowingly false report: A materially false allegation known by the reporting person to be false when made; an unsubstantiated, mistaken or withdrawn allegation is not automatically knowingly false.

5. General No-Unnecessary-Contact Rule

5.1 Instructors shall not initiate unnecessary bodily contact with a student during ordinary instruction.

5.2 Instructors shall not deliberately touch students' hands, wrists, arms, shoulders, back, waist, legs, face, hair or other body parts for routine instruction when non-contact instruction is appropriate.

5.3 Prohibited conduct includes physically repositioning a student's hands or feet during routine instruction; unneeded posture adjustments; hugging, massage, stroking or tickling; physical punishment or restraint; physical expressions of anger or affection; sexual contact; and creating a pretext for contact.

5.4 Attendance, lesson payment, prior familiarity or a failure to object do not establish consent to inappropriate contact.

5.5 A student cannot authorize conduct prohibited by law, ICBC requirements or this Policy merely by appearing to consent.

6. Limited Exceptions

6.1 Limited contact may occur in a mutually welcomed ordinary greeting (such as a handshake), genuinely accidental incidental contact, necessary emergency safety intervention, lawful first aid or medical response, or properly assessed disability-related assistance.

6.2 Contact in these circumstances must remain lawful, appropriate and proportionate. No exception permits sexual, punitive, coercive or otherwise inappropriate touching.

6.3 Instructors shall respect requests not to engage in social touching. Ordinary greetings shall never be required.

7. Instruction Without Touching

7.1 Instructors shall use verbal directions, instructor demonstrations, visual aids, safe stationary-vehicle demonstrations and approved vehicle controls wherever feasible.

7.2 Students should ordinarily adjust their own seat belts, seating positions, mirrors, wheel position and driving posture.

7.3 When assistance is requested or appears necessary, an instructor should explain the task and allow the student to perform it independently when safe.

7.4 If an accommodation could require physical assistance, management shall assess lawful and safe arrangements before continuation, except in an immediate emergency.

8. Authority and Limits

8.1 Instructors may undertake lawful and reasonable measures necessary to address an imminent collision or significant immediate risk.

8.2 Measures may include urgent verbal instructions, operation of fitted dual controls, taking necessary control of the steering wheel, safely stopping the vehicle and other proportionate protective action.

8.3 Where reasonably possible, an effective non-contact method shall be preferred. Bodily contact may occur only to the extent reasonably necessary or unavoidable while responding to immediate danger.

8.4 Contact and intervention shall cease once no longer reasonably necessary.

8.5 An emergency shall not be manufactured or exaggerated to justify touching.

8.6 Poor hand placement, ordinary driving mistakes, nervousness, posture issues or disagreement with feedback alone do not constitute an emergency warranting physical contact.

8.7 Necessity is evaluated objectively against circumstances reasonably apparent at the time, not solely through hindsight or the eventual outcome.

8.8 Contact during an emergency does not automatically demonstrate misconduct; an instructor's claim of emergency likewise does not automatically justify contact.

9. Response After an Incident

9.1 Once safe, the instructor shall stop in a safe location; assess injuries and medical needs; explain events calmly and factually; inform management promptly; and document the event no later than the same calendar day unless reasonably prevented.

9.2 The incident report shall record date, time, approximate location, road and vehicle conditions, the immediate hazard, student and instructor actions, any warnings, nature and approximate duration of contact, witnesses, available evidence and follow-up steps.

9.3 The instructor shall not compel the student to endorse the instructor's account; the student may provide an independent account.

9.4 Management shall assess whether the lesson can continue safely, requires reassignment or must end.

10. Conduct and Communication

10.1 Instructors shall be patient, respectful, professional and attentive to road safety.

10.2 Sexual harassment, sexualized remarks, unwanted propositions, discriminatory statements, threats, humiliation, coercion, intimidation and exploitation of instructor authority are prohibited.

10.3 Constructive feedback must relate to driving performance and be delivered appropriately.

10.4 Instructors shall not solicit or pursue romantic or sexual relationships with current students.

10.5 School communications should concern training, scheduling, payment, safety, progress or legitimate school business and should normally use approved channels.

10.6 Instructors shall not demand private contact channels, send sexual messages, request intimate photographs or avoid school oversight through unauthorized communications.

10.7 Instructors shall not promise a successful ICBC road test in exchange for a personal benefit.

11. Student Standards

11.1 Students shall refrain from physical aggression, unwanted touching, threatening or sexualized conduct, deliberate interference with safe operation, coercion and knowingly fabricated evidence.

11.2 Students must comply with reasonable immediate road-safety instructions.

11.3 Students retain the right to ask questions, dispute fees, report concerns, seek legal advice, refuse inappropriate touching and make lawful external complaints.

11.4 Instructors may stop instruction for a genuine and material safety concern; disputed decisions shall be reviewed and shall not be used as pretexts for retaliation or discrimination.

12. Students Under 19

12.1 Instructors shall maintain heightened, age-appropriate professional boundaries with minors.

12.2 Instructors shall not initiate intimate touching, request secrecy about inappropriate conduct, send suggestive communications or arrange unauthorized social meetings.

12.3 Appropriate guardian communications shall be maintained while respecting the young person's privacy and lawful rights.

12.4 A minor's complaint shall be reviewed whether or not a parent reports it.

12.5 Where there is reason to believe a child needs protection under the Child, Family and Community Service Act, the legally required report shall be made promptly. Parent notification shall not compromise child safety or child-protection processes.

13. Accommodation

13.1 The School shall consider reasonable accommodation as required by BC human-rights law, including disability-related needs.

13.2 Assessment shall consider the student's needs, safe alternatives, instructor capability, adaptive equipment, law and undue-hardship limits where applicable.

13.3 Any physical assistance must be lawfully authorized, specific, explained, necessary and limited to the need. Unnecessary medical disclosure shall not be required.

13.4 A request for accommodation shall not, of itself, result in punishment or automatic exclusion.

14. Student Rights

14.1 Students are entitled to respectful instruction, protection from prohibited contact, reasonable safety explanations, appropriate complaint channels, privacy safeguards, freedom from retaliation and a fair review of concerns.

14.2 Students may request reassignment to another instructor, subject to reasonable availability and arrangements.

14.3 Students may contact ICBC, police, child protection, legal counsel or another competent body without the School's permission.

15. Instructor Fairness

15.1 Allegations shall be considered impartially and without an automatic presumption of guilt or falsity.

15.2 Subject to legal, confidentiality and safety requirements, an instructor shall be informed of the substance of an allegation, allowed to respond, provide evidence and identify witnesses.

15.3 Reasonably necessary interim protective measures may include changed assignments or temporary suspension from instruction; such measures are not findings of guilt.

15.4 Instructors shall not intimidate or pressure complainants or witnesses, and the School may appoint an independent investigator.

16. Receiving Complaints

16.1 Concerns may be reported verbally or in writing by students, guardians, instructors, employees or witnesses.

16.2 Complaints shall not be automatically rejected for delay, lack of video, absence of witnesses, incomplete details, withdrawal or because the respondent disputes them.

16.3 The School should acknowledge ordinary complaints within two business days and prioritize immediate safety risks without delay.

16.4 Allegations concerning management shall be referred to an independent reviewer where reasonably necessary; an external authority may also be contacted.

16.5 The School shall not require mediation, a settlement, a confidentiality commitment or withdrawal of an external report as a condition of taking necessary safety steps.

17. Initial Risk Assessment

17.1 Management shall consider immediate safety, medical care, child protection duties, any appropriate police or ICBC referral, evidence preservation and temporary precautions.

17.2 The School shall preserve relevant information where litigation or regulatory proceedings are reasonably anticipated.

18. Fair Investigation

18.1 Reviews shall be timely, proportionate, impartial and documented.

18.2 Relevant evidence may include independent accounts, witnesses, lesson logs, contemporaneous notes, communications, vehicle and route information, incident reports and lawfully obtained recordings.

18.3 Credibility, consistency, context, corroboration and explanations for inconsistencies shall be evaluated without automatic preference based on student, employee or management status.

18.4 Internal findings shall generally use the balance-of-probabilities standard unless a different standard is legally required.

18.5 Lack of injury, video or immediate reporting shall not alone determine the result. Accidental contact must be distinguished from intentional or reckless misconduct.

18.6 Findings may be substantiated, not substantiated, inconclusive, withdrawn (with appropriate continuing review) or knowingly false where independently supported.

18.7 The reviewer shall document the evidence, analysis, reasons and any recommended measures.

19. Fabrication, Tampering and Retaliation

19.1 Material evidence must not be knowingly fabricated, altered, destroyed or concealed; witnesses must not be threatened.

19.2 A failed or unsubstantiated complaint is not evidence by itself of deliberate fabrication.

19.3 No participant shall be threatened with fees, penalties, lawsuits or other adverse consequences merely for making a good-faith complaint or exercising lawful rights.

19.4 Legitimate safety or discipline decisions may be made on independent documented grounds, not as pretexts for retaliation.

20. Outcomes

20.1 Depending on substantiated findings and law, proportionate responses may include no action, training, coaching, warning, reassignment, suspension, termination or referral to ICBC, police or other authorities.

20.2 The School shall consider protective measures and lawful remedies for affected students.

20.3 Fee decisions and refunds must comply with applicable law and contracts; no benefit shall depend on unlawful secrecy or waiver of rights.

20.4 Outcomes shall be communicated appropriately while respecting privacy and personnel confidentiality.

21. Training Records

21.1 The School shall keep accurate instructional and other records required under ICBC rules and applicable law.

21.2 Applicable driver-training records created on or after June 1, 2021 shall be retained for at least six years as required by Division 27, where that provision applies.

21.3 Records must not be fabricated, backdated or materially altered without a traceable correction including date and reason.

  • Motor Vehicle Act Regulations, section 27.06

22. Incident Records and Preservation

22.1 Incident reports, complaints, findings and supporting records shall be securely retained under a lawful documented retention schedule and accessed only for legitimate purposes.

22.2 Where a claim or investigation is reasonably anticipated, potentially relevant records must be preserved and ordinary deletion suspended as appropriate.

22.3 The School shall collect and retain personal information only for lawful, reasonable purposes, including applicable statutory retention requirements.

23. Recording and Monitoring

23.1 The School shall not assume in-vehicle audio or video recording is automatically lawful.

23.2 Before implementing any recording, it shall assess necessity, proportionality, less intrusive options, notice, legal authority, any required consent, access restrictions, retention and secure deletion.

23.3 Recording shall not be repurposed for entertainment, social-media publication or unrelated monitoring without lawful authority.

23.4 Instructors shall not use concealed personal devices for unauthorized recording. Nothing in this Policy prohibits otherwise lawful evidence gathering or reporting to authorities.

24. Vehicle and Lesson Safety

24.1 Training vehicles shall comply with applicable licensing, inspection, insurance, equipment and road-safety requirements.

24.2 Instructors shall verify required safety equipment is operational before lessons and remain alert to hazards.

24.3 Instructors shall not use handheld phones or engage in distracting personal activities while supervising a moving vehicle.

24.4 Instruction shall cease where a genuine defect, impairment or other condition creates an unacceptable risk; material stoppages shall be documented.

25. Routes, Pickup and Drop-Off

25.1 Pickup, drop-off and deviations shall be reasonably connected to the instruction or genuine safety needs.

25.2 Instructors shall not require visits to private residences, hotel rooms or unrelated premises as a condition of instruction, or arrange unnecessary secluded stops and personal errands.

25.3 Material deviations should be capable of factual explanation.

25.4 If a student feels unsafe, the instructor shall, when consistent with road safety, stop safely and facilitate contact with management or assistance.

26. Training and Acknowledgment

26.1 All instructors shall review this Policy and ICBC's Code of Conduct before conducting instruction and shall confirm understanding in writing.

26.2 Training shall cover no-contact methods, emergency response, professional boundaries, minors, privacy, reporting, evidence and non-retaliation.

26.3 The School shall provide annual refreshers and review relevant standards at onboarding and as required by ICBC.

27. Confidentiality

27.1 Confidential information shall be disclosed only to persons with a legitimate business, safety, legal or investigative need.

27.2 Absolute secrecy cannot be promised when disclosure is required for legal compliance, fairness, investigation or public safety.

27.3 Lawful communications to ICBC, police, child-protection authorities, courts, tribunals and counsel shall not be obstructed.

28. Administration

28.1 Management shall apply this Policy reasonably, consistently and without unlawful discrimination.

28.2 If any provision is unenforceable, the remainder shall continue to the extent permitted by law.

28.3 The School may revise the Policy for legal, regulatory or operational developments; material changes shall be communicated to affected parties.

28.4 Changes shall not retroactively waive statutory rights or excuse unlawful conduct.

Schedule A: Instructor acknowledgement

I acknowledge that I have received and read this Policy; understand the prohibition on inappropriate physical contact and harassment; understand that emergency intervention must be necessary and proportionate; will document reportable events accurately and promptly; will respect privacy, professional boundaries and legal reporting obligations; and understand that allegations will be assessed fairly and may lead to lawful action. This acknowledgment does not waive statutory rights or duties.

Schedule B: Student safety acknowledgement

I acknowledge that I have been informed of the School's physical-contact, professional-boundaries and emergency-intervention rules. I understand that instructors ordinarily shall not initiate unnecessary physical contact, but reasonable urgent steps to prevent imminent danger can involve limited incidental bodily contact. I understand I may raise concerns without retaliation; disputes will be reviewed on relevant evidence; and I must follow reasonable immediate road-safety instructions. This acknowledgment does not authorize inappropriate touching, release anyone from legal liability or waive my legal rights.
