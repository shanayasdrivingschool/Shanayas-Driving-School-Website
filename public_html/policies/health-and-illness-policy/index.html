Health, Illness Prevention & Fitness-to-Drive Policy

When illness or impairment affects a lesson, how to reschedule, and how the school handles safety, accommodation and health privacy.

Published policy

Effective: April 1, 2026

Shanaya’s Driving School’s Health, Illness Prevention & Fitness-to-Drive Policy (SDS-HS-002, version 1.1) explains when a lesson should pause or move, what happens to fees, and how health information and accommodation requests are handled.

  • Short-notice illness rescheduling
  • Health privacy and accommodation

1. Purpose and commitment

1.1 The School shall take reasonable steps to maintain a safe, healthy and respectful environment for students, instructors and others affected by driver training.

1.2 This Policy governs communicable-illness precautions, fitness for safe driving and supervision, illness-related lesson interruptions, cancellations, reasonable accommodation, privacy and incident reporting.

1.3 Neither students nor instructors shall be pressured to proceed when there is an objectively supportable, material health or road-safety risk. The School shall apply these provisions fairly, proportionately and without unlawful discrimination.

2. Fitness to participate and limited disclosure

2.1 Before instruction, each participant shall reasonably assess whether current symptoms, fatigue, medication effects or temporary impairment may materially compromise safe participation or expose others to a significant communicable-disease risk.

2.2 A person aware of a material immediate safety concern shall notify the School as soon as reasonably practicable, without being required to disclose unrelated medical diagnoses or a complete medical history.

2.3 The School may request only reasonably necessary information about functional capacity, immediate risk or an accommodation need, subject to applicable privacy, employment and human-rights law.

3. Conditions requiring postponement or discontinuation

3.1 A lesson shall not begin or continue where an instructor reasonably identifies an unmanaged material safety risk due to: (a) fever with symptoms of infectious illness; (b) significant or worsening potentially contagious respiratory symptoms; (c) vomiting or diarrhea suspected to be infectious; (d) dizziness, fainting, confusion or altered consciousness; (e) severe drowsiness or fatigue; (f) materially impairing medication or substance effects; or (g) another immediate condition affecting safe driving or supervision.

3.2 Stable, non-contagious symptoms and chronic conditions, including ordinary seasonal allergies, shall not automatically disqualify a person. Decisions must rest on individual functional capacity and legitimate safety considerations.

3.3 Emergency medical needs take priority over continuation of instruction.

4. Communicable illness precautions and return

4.1 A person experiencing symptoms consistent with contagious illness should not attend in-person training while unwell and potentially infectious.

4.2 As an internal precaution for respiratory illness, a participant should return when symptoms are improving, they are well enough to participate safely, and any fever has resolved for at least 24 hours without fever-reducing medication. This is a School standard, not a universal ICBC requirement.

4.3 For vomiting or diarrhea consistent with suspected norovirus, the School shall ordinarily require at least 48 hours after symptoms stop before resuming lessons, consistent with public-health guidance.

4.4 Applicable public-health orders, case-specific medical direction and mandatory legal requirements override the School’s general precautionary thresholds.

4.5 Reasonable additional precautions may include hand hygiene, ventilation, cleaning, or an appropriate face covering, without automatically excluding persons with disabilities or stable non-infectious symptoms.

  • B.C. Centre for Disease Control: Respiratory illness guidance

5. Instructor illness and School responsibilities

5.1 An instructor shall not provide driver training when unable to supervise safely due to illness, impairment or severe fatigue, and shall notify management as soon as reasonably practicable.

5.2 Where instructor illness prevents service, the School shall offer a suitable replacement instructor, if available and acceptable, or reschedule without additional charge to the student.

5.3 Fees for undelivered services shall be treated in accordance with the written enrolment contract and applicable law; the School shall not charge for an instructor-cancelled lesson that was not delivered.

5.4 The School shall comply with statutory employee illness or injury leave, including applicable paid and unpaid leave and protections against retaliation. A contractor label shall not override a person’s actual legal employment status.

6. Student illness and participation

6.1 Students shall make reasonable efforts not to attend while knowingly unable to drive safely or while presenting a significant communicable-disease risk, and shall notify the School promptly if illness affects attendance.

6.2 An instructor may pause or postpone instruction based on reasonable, objective and documented safety grounds, not assumptions about disability or diagnosis.

6.3 No student shall be required to remain in an unsafe lesson solely because a booking was prepaid or has begun.

6.4 The School shall consider legally required reasonable accommodation and appropriate alternatives on an individual basis.

7. Illness-related cancellations, credits and refunds

7.1 Students are requested to provide at least 24 hours’ notice of a foreseeable illness-related absence where reasonably practicable. This notice period is a School contractual rule and is not stated to be imposed by ICBC.

7.2 As a standard goodwill provision, the School shall ordinarily permit one genuinely sudden, short-notice illness-related rescheduling without an additional cancellation charge. Subsequent requests shall receive individualized review, particularly where disability or protected accommodation is relevant.

7.3 A fee may be assessed only if lawfully authorized by clearly disclosed, applicable enrolment terms and not inconsistent with human-rights, consumer or other mandatory protections. No fee shall be imposed as punishment for reporting illness or exercising a protected right.

7.4 Routine short-term illness shall not automatically require a medical certificate. Any request for supporting information must be necessary, proportionate and legally permissible.

7.5 The School may review objectively documented patterns of cancellation, but repeated illness, a disputed report or an unsubstantiated concern shall not alone establish dishonesty.

7.6 The School shall disclose applicable cancellation, refund and fee terms in writing before instruction commences and before accepting payment, as required by the B.C. Motor Vehicle Act Regulations, Division 27.

8. Illness arising during a lesson

8.1 If illness, dizziness, impairment or other significant symptoms emerge during instruction, the instructor shall direct the vehicle to a safe stopping location as soon as reasonably practicable.

8.2 Instruction shall be paused and discontinued where continuing presents a material risk. The instructor shall notify management and make reasonable arrangements for safe assistance or transportation, considering whether the student is a minor.

8.3 Emergency services shall be contacted when medically appropriate. A student shall not be abandoned in circumstances creating a foreseeable danger.

8.4 A material interruption shall be documented, and unused lesson time, rescheduling and payment treatment shall be assessed reasonably under the disclosed contract and applicable law.

9. Medication, substances and impairment

9.1 No person shall operate or supervise a training vehicle while materially impaired by alcohol, cannabis, medication or another substance.

9.2 A lawful prescription or medical diagnosis is not proof of impairment. Assessments shall address actual function and safety risk; only necessary information may be requested.

9.3 Where there are reasonable grounds to believe a participant is unfit to drive or supervise, the lesson shall be suspended pending a safe resolution.

10. Hygiene, ventilation and cleaning

10.1 The School shall maintain reasonable hygiene procedures for training vehicles, including appropriate cleaning of high-touch surfaces and access to hand-hygiene supplies.

10.2 Vehicles shall be ventilated where reasonably practicable, subject to weather, safety, and accommodation needs.

10.3 Any contamination by bodily fluids shall be cleaned and disinfected using appropriate precautions before the affected area is reused.

10.4 Mandatory public-health directions shall be implemented where applicable.

11. Privacy and confidential health information

11.1 Health information is sensitive personal information and shall be collected, used, disclosed, secured and retained in accordance with BC’s Personal Information Protection Act and other applicable law.

11.2 Access shall be limited to persons with a legitimate safety, scheduling, accommodation, employment, investigation or legal need.

11.3 The School shall ordinarily seek information about functional restrictions rather than a diagnosis, and shall not publicly identify or disclose a person’s illness without lawful authority.

12. Honesty, evidence and non-retaliation

12.1 All parties shall communicate in good faith. Knowingly falsified material records, fabricated medical documentation, deliberate evidence tampering or intentional misrepresentation to secure an improper benefit are prohibited.

12.2 A report is not knowingly false solely because it is unverified, disputed, withdrawn or unsupported by independent documents. Any adverse finding shall require reliable evidence and fair review.

12.3 Retaliation for genuine illness reporting, requesting legally protected accommodation, asserting employment rights or raising good-faith safety concerns is prohibited.

12.4 Nothing prevents legitimate, non-retaliatory action based on independently documented safety or contractual grounds.

13. Complaints and dispute review

13.1 A student or instructor may request review of an illness-related safety decision, cancellation charge, accommodation response or privacy concern.

13.2 The School shall consider relevant records, notice, the immediate safety issue, the enrolment agreement, potential accommodation duties and the accounts of affected parties.

13.3 A person may contact ICBC, WorkSafeBC, the BC Human Rights Tribunal or another competent authority without prior School permission.

13.4 The School shall preserve material records when a complaint or proceeding is reasonably anticipated and limit disclosure consistently with privacy law.

14. Fitness-to-drive incident records

14.1 A significant illness-related lesson interruption shall be recorded promptly and factually, ordinarily on the same day.

14.2 The incident record should state date, time, location, observed functional concerns, safe-stop actions, any medical response, management notifications and proposed lesson disposition; unnecessary medical diagnoses shall be omitted.

14.3 Incident records shall not be altered or destroyed improperly and shall be subject to the School’s lawful retention schedule.

15. Training, communication and review

15.1 The School shall provide this Policy to instructors, make relevant sections available to students before training, and train personnel in safe-stop, illness cancellation, accommodation, privacy and escalation procedures.

15.2 The Policy shall be reviewed at least annually and after material changes to applicable law, ICBC requirements or public-health directions.

15.3 Instructors shall acknowledge receipt and understanding. Student acknowledgments confirm notice only; they do not constitute health-related consent, liability waivers or releases of statutory rights.

16. ICBC compliance and road safety

16.1 The School and its instructors shall comply with the ICBC Code of Conduct for Driver Training Licensees, the Motor Vehicle Act, Division 27 of the Motor Vehicle Act Regulations and applicable licensing conditions.

16.2 No instructor shall conduct instruction while unable to safely supervise because of illness, fatigue, medication effects or substance impairment.

16.3 No student shall knowingly be permitted to operate the training vehicle where reasonable, objective grounds indicate material impairment or an unmanaged serious safety risk.

16.4 A disputed decision to discontinue an unsafe lesson shall be documented and reviewed fairly.

  • ICBC Driving School Code of Conduct
  • B.C. Motor Vehicle Act Regulations, Division 27

17. Mandatory cancellation and refund disclosures

17.1 The School shall disclose applicable cancellation, rescheduling, refund and fee provisions in writing in advance, consistently with section 27.06(2) of the Motor Vehicle Act Regulations.

17.2 Applicable charges must be supported by lawful disclosed terms and assessed in good faith; they shall not penalize protected sick leave, a legally required disability accommodation or other non-waivable rights.

17.3 No student shall be charged for an instructor-cancelled, undelivered lesson. Appropriate credits or refunds must be documented.

18. BC workplace and employment-law requirements

18.1 The School shall comply with applicable Workers Compensation Act, WorkSafeBC and Employment Standards Act obligations, including appropriate disease-risk controls and protected employee leaves.

18.2 Where section 49.2 of the Employment Standards Act prohibits requiring a specified sick note for a qualifying short employee health-related leave, the School shall not require one. Legally permitted fitness-for-work assessments remain subject to applicable safeguards.

18.3 Employee and contractor classifications shall be determined according to applicable law, not merely contract labels.

  • B.C. Employment Standards Act, section 49.2

19. Human rights, statutory priority and final provisions

19.1 Health and safety decisions shall not be founded on stereotypes about disability, medication or chronic conditions; reasonable accommodation shall be considered in accordance with the Human Rights Code.

19.2 Mandatory law, applicable public-health orders and ICBC licensing rules prevail over conflicting School provisions.

19.3 Nothing in this document waives a non-waivable legal right or limits lawful external reporting.

19.4 This Policy is effective April 1, 2026. It is not a substitute for individualized advice from qualified BC legal counsel.
